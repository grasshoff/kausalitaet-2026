Regularity theory of causal relevance · Narratives

Causality.
Eight cases, one definition.

The Manchester hooters sound every day at five, and in London the workers go home; ice-cream sales and drownings rise together; one says a short circuit caused a fire, although short circuits are neither sufficient nor necessary for fires. The regularity theory of causal relevance after Graßhoff and May uses a single definition to distinguish the factors that are causally relevant to an effect from those that merely occur together with it regularly.

Gerd Graßhoff, Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin, Research Institute of the Deutsches Museum

The theory goes back to Graßhoff and May (2001) and May (1999). The three volumes are working drafts (as of 29 September 2026) and are provided here for discussion.

One definition

Four terms, each resting on the one before.

  1. 1

    Factor

    A kind of circumstance that in each single case is either present or absent. The effect, too, is a factor.

    Example "short circuit", "oxygen present", "fire"

  2. 2

    Cluster

    An and-combination of conditions ("factor is present" or "factor is absent") that is sufficient for the effect. If one condition is struck out, it no longer is.

    Example short circuit and oxygen present, for the fire

  3. 3

    Minimal theory

    An or-combination of clusters that is necessary for the effect: in every case with the effect one cluster holds. If one cluster is struck out, a case with the effect remains in which no other cluster holds.

    Example short circuit with oxygen, or flammable liquid with spark and oxygen

4

A factor is causally relevant to an effect if and only if, for every set of factors that contains it and the effect, there is a minimal theory of the effect formed from that set in which its presence or its absence is a condition of a cluster.

A finding about the recorded factors supports the proposition that a factor is causally relevant but does not prove it: further factors can refute it. Causal relevance is not a degree.
